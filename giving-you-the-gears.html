These are ordinary meshes. 1:1 reverses the spin and keeps the torque. 2:1 doubles torque and halves speed. 3:1, 16 teeth into 48, is a common hobby reduction. 4:1 is about as far as one mesh should go. Two 3:1 meshes in a row make 9:1, nine times the torque at one ninth the speed. Helical is a 20° pair with enough face that one tooth stays in mesh. Herringbone is that same idea in a V, so the push along the shaft cancels. 90° bevel is 20 teeth into 40 with the shafts at a right angle. Rack is 10 straight teeth under a 20-tooth pinion. Internal is a 20-tooth pinion inside a 60-tooth ring. Worm is a single-start screw driving a 30-tooth wheel, 30:1, with the shafts at 90°.

    Solid turns the same parts in space. Drag to look around, or jump to the top, bottom, front, or side. The working tooth is an involute. A helix leans that tooth through the thickness, and a herringbone mirrors the lean so the shaft is not pushed endwise. A 90° bevel tapers toward the point where the shafts meet, and the large end sits on the printer bed. A rack is straight teeth on a bar. An internal ring has the teeth pointing inward, with a rim outside the roots. A worm is a screw; its wheel is a helical gear at the screw's lead angle, and the two shafts are at 90°. The hole stays straight, so a round or hex shaft still slides through. Below the base circle the root drops straight to the gullet, rather than a hobbed fillet. Add a little backlash, about 0.1–0.2 mm, when a printed pair comes out tight. One spur mesh is happiest between about 1:1 and 5:1. For more, stack meshes, or use a worm: a single start into 30 teeth is 30:1.
